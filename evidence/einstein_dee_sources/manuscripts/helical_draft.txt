      Helical Perturbations in Finite-Matter Toroidal–Helical Transport: Nonlinear
                     Distortion, Stability, and Dynamical Recovery
                                                   Gerald T. Dahlberg Jr.1
                      1
                          Theoretical and Computational Physics Researcher, Sacramento, California, USA
                                                    (Dated: August 24, 2026)
               We formulate a nonlinear perturbation framework for a finite-matter toroidal–helical transport
            background in which the imposed disturbance is characterized explicitly by its amplitude, axial
            propagation, azimuthal winding, characteristic frequency, and mode number. The aim is to iden-
            tify parameter ranges in which a helical perturbation undergoes nonlinear wave distortion while
            remaining bounded, and to determine whether, where, and under what conditions the distorted
            configuration dynamically recovers toward a reference state. The perturbation is treated as a cou-
            pled axial–azimuthal structure rather than as a purely radial or purely axial disturbance, allowing
            the winding geometry and propagation scale to be varied independently. Stability is assessed through
            boundedness, excursion magnitude, nonlinear waveform deformation, growth or decay of the pertur-
            bation envelope, and recovery after finite displacement. The resulting parameter map is intended
            to distinguish bounded-recovery, marginal, and unstable regimes and to provide explicit initial-data
            targets for subsequent nonlinear Einstein–matter evolution. The present analysis does not prescribe
            the outcome of the full nonlinear evolution; it defines the perturbative variables, diagnostics, and
            parameter regimes needed to test that evolution systematically.
