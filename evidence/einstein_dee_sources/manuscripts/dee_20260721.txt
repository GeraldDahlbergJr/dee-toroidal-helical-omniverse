                                               Abstract
    We formulate a coupled radial-response and toroidal-helical transport model intended to test
whether a finite-separation equilibrium can arise dynamically rather than being imposed as a
kinematic constraint. The model is built from a covariant action containing a nonlinear radial
scalar, two transport phase fields, and a field-dependent kinetic mixing. We define a Dynamic
Equilibrium Event (DEE) as a finite, locally stable stationary point of the radial sector under
transport backreaction. For a minimal polynomial realization, we obtain a closed-form finite-
separation branch and show that its radial stability follows directly from the equilibrium condition.
We derive the matter stress-energy tensor and prove that its null contraction is nonnegative for
every null vector whenever the two-field kinetic matrix is positive definite. We then study an
explicit non-oscillatory transport configuration and solve the cylindrically symmetric linearized
Einstein equations for a localized source. After a positive source rescaling of 10−6 , the largest
metric perturbation is of order 10−3 , while the tested co-rotating and counter-rotating null-energy
contractions remain positive. Exploratory reduced-metric calculations are also reported to show why
prescribing off-diagonal geometry without a compatible matter source can generate energy-condition
violations. The present result is a consistent weak-field construction and a set of nonlinear initial-data
targets; it is not yet a proof of global non-collapse in the full Einstein-matter system.

I.    INTRODUCTION


     Gravitational collapse is ordinarily analyzed by specifying a matter model, solving the
Einstein equations, and determining whether trapped surfaces, horizons, or singular behavior
form. A recurring difficulty in speculative non-collapse constructions is that positive sep-
aration is imposed directly through the geometry. Such a construction can demonstrate
non-intersection by design but cannot establish that separation emerges from dynamics.

     The objective of this work is narrower and testable. We ask whether a radial collapse
degree of freedom can be coupled to directed toroidal and helical transport so that a finite
equilibrium branch appears as a solution of the field equations of an explicit matter model.
We refer to the transition into such a state as a Dynamic Equilibrium Event. The term
’event’ denotes a dynamical transition, not a new causal boundary.

     Two transport regimes are distinguished. The first is a nonlinear radial response, rep-
resented by a scalar field whose effective force changes with its local state. The second is
linear non-waveform transport: directed flow represented by constant or slowly varying field
gradients rather than by a sinusoidal carrier. ’Linear’ therefore describes the transport profile,
not the complete Einstein-matter equations, which remain nonlinear.

     The manuscript proceeds from a covariant action, derives the matter equations and
stress tensor, solves a minimal finite-separation branch, and proves a null-energy condition
for the matter sector. An explicit localized configuration is then used as a source for the
linearized cylindrical Einstein equations. Earlier exploratory metric ansatze are retained only
as diagnostic calculations, because they reveal how unsupported off-diagonal geometry may
demand inadmissible stress-energy.

     The principal contribution is therefore not a claim of completed nonlinear non-collapse. It
is an internally consistent weak-field framework that identifies the mathematical conditions a
full nonlinear DEE solution must satisfy.

      This work does not present a global solution of the full nonlinear Einstein-matter equations.

values and require independent reproduction with a documented numerical code before
journal submission. The weak-field solution should be understood as a consistency checkpoint
and initial-data seed.

X.     DISCUSSION


     The development above separates three questions that are often conflated. First, can a
finite radial branch exist? Equation (18) answers yes within the model. Second, can that
branch be locally stable? Equation (21) answers yes in the homogeneous radial sector. Third,
can the supporting matter satisfy the NEC? Proposition 1 answers yes whenever the kinetic
matrix is positive definite.
     Those statements do not settle the gravitational question. The Einstein equations can
generate additional instabilities, constraint violations, trapped surfaces, or curvature growth.
The weak-field calculation shows that there is no immediate inconsistency at sufficiently
small source amplitude, but the nonlinear regime is where the central hypothesis must be
tested.
     The distinction between nonlinear radial response and linear non-waveform transport
is useful because it assigns different roles to the degrees of freedom. The radial scalar
determines the local separation branch, while the transport gradients carry angular and
helical momentum. Their coupling changes the radial effective force without requiring an
independently postulated centrifugal term.



     A.   Relation to angular stress


     In the local orthonormal frame, the counter-rotating null direction is controlled by ϵ+p_ϕ-
2q_ϕ. A prescribed metric shift can make q_ϕ too large compared with the supporting
energy and pressure. In the action-derived model, the full null contraction is instead the
quadratic form in Eq. (29), and positive definiteness of the kinetic matrix protects every null
direction.

                                              13
   B.    Falsifiability


   The framework can fail in several concrete ways: the initial constraints may have no regular
solution in the desired parameter region; the finite branch may destabilize under coupled
metric perturbations; the solution may develop trapped surfaces or divergent curvature; or
the NEC may fail after nonlinear backreaction. Any of these outcomes would falsify the
strong non-collapse hypothesis for the tested model.

XII.    CONCLUSION


   We have constructed a covariant coupled-field model in which a radial nonlinear degree
of freedom interacts with toroidal and helical transport gradients. A minimal polynomial
realization admits a closed-form finite branch, and every positive point on that branch
is locally stable in the radial scalar sector. The matter stress tensor satisfies the NEC
for all null directions whenever its kinetic matrix is positive definite. An explicit localized
source produces a consistent cylindrical linearized Einstein response after a positive amplitude

                                             14
rescaling, with metric perturbations of order 10−3 and positive tested co- and counter-rotating
directional NEC contractions.
   The present work therefore establishes a mathematically coherent weak-field DEE frame-
work. It does not establish global non-collapse. The next decisive calculation is a constraint-
preserving nonlinear Einstein-matter evolution that monitors the minimum separation, full
null cone, curvature invariants, trapped-surface diagnostics, and convergence of the con-
straints.
